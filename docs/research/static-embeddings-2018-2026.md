# Static and linear sentence embeddings after SIF, uSIF and SWEM (2018 to 2026)

Model terms apply separately from fse: the licenses of paranmt and paragram are unknown. GloVe uses PDDL, word2vec uses Apache-2.0, fastText vectors use CC BY-SA 3.0, and POTION uses MIT.

Research report for fse 2.x. Date of research: 2026-10-27. Superseded in part on 2026-10-29: training left fse (no `fit`, no `counts`, no `remove_components@1` or `project@1`); fse 2.0 encodes finished tables with `weighted_mean@1` and an optional `normalize@1`. Recommendations below that build on `fit`, `counts` or those steps (items 3 to 5 of section 6) now mean offline recipes that write finished tables. Scope: non-transformer sentence encoders whose inference is a lookup of per-token vectors plus a cheap pooling, and post-processing of such vectors.

Conventions:

- "Verified" means I read the number or fact in the primary source (paper PDF, repository code, model files on the Hugging Face Hub) during this research. Everything else is marked **(unverified)**.
- MTEB averages from different sources are **not comparable with each other**: each project evaluates its own task subset and MTEB version. Comparisons are only made within one table.
- fse's own architecture terms follow docs/architecture/overview.md, "Principle and stages": stage 1 tokenize, stage 2 token rows and factors (per-word weight), stage 3 `Aggregation` (`weighted_mean@1`), stage 4 steps (`remove_components@1`, `project@1`, `normalize@1`), stage 5 storage. Linear steps are folded into the word vectors.

## 1. Summary

1. The quality frontier for static sentence embeddings since 2024 is **distilled or contrastively trained token tables with mean pooling**: Model2Vec/POTION (MinishLab, 2024 to 2025), Sentence Transformers `StaticEmbedding` models (January 2025), WordLlama (2024), Wada et al. (EMNLP 2025), and in 2026 SSE (RikkaBotan) and bekko-static-embedding-v1 (hotchpotch, October 2026). All of them compute exactly `mean(token rows)` (optionally then L2 normalize) at inference; I verified this in the code or model files of model2vec, Sentence Transformers, WordLlama and swe4semantics. That is fse's `weighted_mean@1` (+ `normalize@1`) with factor 1. What differs is **the tokenizer** (WordPiece, Unigram or Llama BPE instead of words) and **how the table was made**.
2. In fse's own baseline (`benchmarks/baseline/README.md`, 35 MTEB tasks, single run), potion-base-8M beats fse uSIF paranmt-300 by 4.6 points on Classification, 3.3 on Clustering, 4.6 on PairClassification, 3.3 on Reranking and 3.7 on Retrieval, and ties on STS (72.45 vs 72.12) and Summarization (25.10 vs 25.11). This is the strongest in-house evidence that loading such tables matters more than any new aggregation.
3. Classic unsupervised improvements published 2018 to 2020 (p-means, GEM, DynaMax, DCT, P-SIF, random encoders, conceptor negation) gave modest or task-specific gains, several are pairwise or order-dependent and therefore outside fse's per-sentence, frozen design. Two post-processing methods stand out because they fit fse's linear algebra exactly: **Zipfian whitening** (NeurIPS 2024) and **conceptor negation** (AAAI 2019), the latter being expressible as `remove_components@1` with weights.
4. No new *word-vector training architecture* after fastText (2016/2017) became a standard. The static line continued through (a) distillation from contextual models (Bommasani et al. ACL 2020, Gupta and Jaggi ACL 2021, Model2Vec 2024, Wada et al. EMNLP 2025) and (b) contrastive training of embedding bags with sentence-transformer recipes (2025 to 2026). The only refresh of a classic architecture is GloVe 2024 (report July 2025), same algorithm, new corpora.

## 2. Distilled and trained static models (most relevant)

### 2.1 Model2Vec and POTION (MinishLab, 2024 to 2025)

- **What it computes.** Distillation: every vocabulary token of a sentence transformer is passed through the model (now with mean pooling over the output), the vectors are reduced by PCA, and weighted by SIF `a / (a + p)` with `p` estimated from Zipf's law (earlier: Zipf weight `log(1/rank)`). Inference: tokenize with the teacher's Hugging Face tokenizer without special tokens, drop `[UNK]`, look up rows, multiply by optional per-token `weights`, mean, optional L2 normalize. Verified in `model2vec/model.py` (`tokenize`, `_encode_helper`, `_encode_batch`). POTION adds Tokenlearn pre-training (2.2).
- **Sources.** Repository [MinishLab/model2vec](https://github.com/MinishLab/model2vec) (MIT); blog posts [POTION, 2024-10-29](https://minish.ai/blog/2024-10-29-tokenlearn-blogpost/) and [distillation improvements, 2025-02-05](https://minish.ai/blog/2025-02-05-improvements/). There is no peer-reviewed paper; the project asks to cite it as software (2024). I found no arXiv paper.
- **Models (verified on the Hub, all MIT).** `minishlab/potion-base-8M` (2024-10-29; 29,528 x 256 F32; WordPiece, BertNormalizer lowercase), `potion-base-32M` (2025-01-22; 63,091 x 512; WordPiece with added whole words), `potion-retrieval-32M` (2025-01-23; 63,091 x 512), `potion-multilingual-128M` (2025-05-22; 500,353 x 256; SentencePiece Unigram from bge-m3, Metaspace pre-tokenizer, precompiled normalizer). Each `model.safetensors` holds one tensor `embeddings`; no separate `weights` tensor, so the weighting is folded into the rows. All four configs say `"normalize": true`.
- **Quality.** model2vec `results/README.md` (their MTEB subset, "Avg (MTEB)"): all-MiniLM-L6-v2 55.93, potion-base-32M 52.13, potion-base-8M 51.08, M2V_base_output 47.96, GloVe_300d 45.82, BPEmb_50k_300d 41.74. STS column: MiniLM 78.95, potion-32M 73.93, potion-8M 72.91, GloVe 61.52. Ablation (2025-02-05 post, Avg MTEB): base 45.34, + mean pooling 45.91, + SIF weighting 47.40, + normalization 47.79. fse's own baseline numbers versus uSIF are in section 1, point 2.
- **Speed claims.** "up to 500 times faster on CPU than the original model", distillation "~30 seconds on a CPU" (README). Sentence Transformers measured potion-base-8M at 124,030 sentences/s on CPU versus 1,739 for all-MiniLM-L6-v2 ([HF blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/static-embeddings)). Speed is mostly tokenizer-bound; fse's baseline already reaches 3.2 million sentences/s for Average on 10 workers with its own tokenizer.
- **Fit for fse.**
  - Stage: **new word vectors on the hub plus a tokenizer.** Rows map 1:1 to vocabulary strings (`##ing` is just a byte string), weights are folded (factor 1), aggregation is `weighted_mean@1`, then `normalize@1`. No new aggregation or step.
  - Tokenizer: `fse_words@1` cannot produce WordPiece pieces. With `external` the caller would pass the token strings from the Hugging Face tokenizer. A native identifier (for example `bert_wordpiece_uncased@1`: BertNormalizer clean text, CJK spacing, lowercase and, per Hugging Face convention when `strip_accents` is null and `lowercase` is true, accent stripping **(unverified in source)**; BertPreTokenizer; greedy longest-match WordPiece with `[UNK]` above 100 characters) would cover potion-base-*, potion-retrieval-32M and static-retrieval-mrl-en-v1. The multilingual model needs a Unigram/SentencePiece tokenizer with a precompiled NFKC-style charsmap: much larger effort.
  - Parity details that a converter must handle: model2vec drops `[UNK]` (fse: drop the `[UNK]` row, then unknown pieces are "no token", identical semantics); `encode` truncates by default to 512 tokens and first cuts the string to `512 * median_token_length` characters (verified in `model.py`), while fse never truncates, so long texts differ by design; numpy `mean` uses pairwise summation, so results agree within float32 rounding, not bit for bit. Newer model2vec models may carry `weights` and a `token_mapping` (vocabulary quantization by clustering); fse can fold `weights` into the factor (stage 2 already has `weight[w]`), and a mapping can be expanded into duplicated rows or would need a new tensor.
  - Frozen, bit-reproducible, CPU-fast: yes, fully.
  - Effort: converter and hub upload small (days); native WordPiece tokenizer medium (needs NFD/accent tables and a reference in `fse._reference_text`).

### 2.2 Tokenlearn (MinishLab, 2024; 0.2.0 on 2025-05-31)

- **What it computes.** Pre-training of a Model2Vec table: a teacher embeds about 1M C4 texts once; the static student is trained to match them (2024: cosine distance plus strong regularization, then PCA and SIF reweighting with corpus probabilities `1e-3/(1e-3+p)`; 0.2.0: MSE, token embeddings and token **norms optimized separately**, optional vocabulary extension with frequent words). Sources: [repo](https://github.com/MinishLab/tokenlearn) (MIT), [2024 post](https://minish.ai/blog/2024-10-29-tokenlearn-blogpost/), [0.2.0 post](https://minish.ai/blog/2025-05-31-tokenlearn-release/).
- **Quality.** 2024 post (their MTEB subset): potion-base-8M 50.03, potion-base-4M 48.23, potion-base-2M 44.77, M2V_base_output 45.34, GloVe_300d 42.36, all-MiniLM-L6-v2 56.09. The 0.2.0 post says potion-multilingual-128M performs "about the same" as static-similarity-mrl-multilingual-v1, without numbers.
- **Fit.** This is a *training* tool (torch, teacher model, GPU). It produces word vectors that fse would load (2.1). The separation of norms and directions in 0.2.0 matches fse's separation of stage-2 weights from stored rows exactly: a learned per-token weight could be shipped as fse weights instead of folded. Implementing Tokenlearn inside fse is out of scope for a frozen-encoder library.

### 2.3 Sentence Transformers static embeddings (Tom Aarsen, 2025-01-15)

- **What it computes.** `StaticEmbedding` is a torch `EmbeddingBag` with mean pooling over Hugging Face tokenizer ids (`add_special_tokens=False`, `[UNK]` kept as a row; verified in `sentence_transformers/sentence_transformer/modules/static_embedding.py`). Trained from random initialization with `MultipleNegativesRankingLoss` inside `MatryoshkaLoss` (dims 32 to 1024), batch 2048, one epoch. Source: [HF blog "Train 400x faster Static Embedding Models"](https://huggingface.co/blog/static-embeddings).
- **Models (verified, Apache-2.0).** `sentence-transformers/static-retrieval-mrl-en-v1` (30,522 x 1024 F32, bert-base-uncased tokenizer) and `static-similarity-mrl-multilingual-v1` (105,879 x 1024, bert-base-multilingual-uncased). `modules.json` has only the `StaticEmbedding` module: no Normalize module, cosine at use.
- **Quality and speed (blog, NanoBEIR NDCG@10, sentences/s CPU).** static-retrieval-mrl-en-v1 0.5032 at 107,420/s; all-MiniLM-L6-v2 0.5623 at 1,739/s; bge-base-en-v1.5 0.6376 at 265/s; potion-base-8M 0.4421 at 124,030/s; glove.6B.300d 0.3293 at 76,520/s; BM25 0.4518. Multilingual model: about 125x faster on CPU than multilingual-e5-small with about 8 % loss (blog). Matryoshka: halving dimensions costs about 1.47 % NDCG@10 for retrieval.
- **Fit.** Same as 2.1: new word vectors plus WordPiece tokenizer, `weighted_mean@1`, then `normalize@1` (fse's default ends with normalize; cosine is unchanged by it). Matryoshka truncation is a linear projection onto the first k coordinates: already expressible as `project@1` with unit basis rows, followed by `normalize@1`; a dedicated `truncate@k` step would be cheaper but is not required. To keep `[UNK]` semantics, the converter keeps the `[UNK]` row and the tokenizer must emit `[UNK]`. Effort: small once a WordPiece tokenizer exists. Frozen and CPU-fast: yes.

### 2.4 WordLlama (D. Lee Miller, 2024)

- **What it computes.** Token embedding codebooks extracted from LLMs (Llama 2/3 family; "L2 supercat" concatenates several Llama-2-tokenizer models), then a small projection is trained; the shipped file is the projected table. Inference: Llama 2 tokenizer without special tokens, masked average, optional normalize (verified in `src/wordllama/inference.py`). Default weights `l2_supercat_256.safetensors`: one tensor `embedding.weight`, F16, 32,000 x 256 (verified). Source: [dleemiller/WordLlama](https://github.com/dleemiller/WordLlama), MIT.
- **Quality (README, own MTEB subset).** WL256: Clustering 33.25, Reranking 52.03, Classification 58.21, PairClassification 78.22, STS 67.91; GloVe 300d: 27.73, 43.29, 57.29, 70.92, 61.85; all-MiniLM-L6-v2: 42.35, 58.04, 63.05, 82.37, 78.90. Wada et al. 2025 (below) report WordLlama MTEB Avg 50.23 versus Model2Vec (potion-base-32M) 51.33 on their benchmark.
- **Speed.** Only in a chart in the README; no numbers in text **(unverified)**.
- **Fit.** New word vectors (float16 exists in fse), `weighted_mean@1`, `normalize@1`. Tokenizer: SentencePiece BPE with byte fallback (Llama 2), not WordPiece; needs `external` or another native tokenizer. Lower priority than POTION, which scores higher.

### 2.5 Static Word Embeddings for Sentence Semantic Representation (Wada et al., EMNLP 2025)

- **What it computes.** Word-level (150k English words) static vectors: each word's contextual vector from GTE-base averaged over 100 sentences containing it; **sentence-level PCA** (average word vectors over 100k sentences, drop the first `floor(d/100)` components as in All-but-the-Top, keep the next 256 or 512); then knowledge distillation of the teacher's in-batch similarity distribution (or contrastive learning on translation pairs for cross-lingual vectors). Inference: plain average of word vectors, then L2 normalize; tokens from the BERT pre-tokenizer, punctuation dropped, case-sensitive lookup with a lowercase fallback, unknown words backed off by dropping trailing subwords (verified in `util.py` of the repository). Sources: [arXiv 2506.04624](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04624), [ACL Anthology 2025.emnlp-main.316](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.316), [twadada/swe4semantics](https://github.com/twadada/swe4semantics) (code and English, en-de, en-zh vectors Apache-2.0; en-ja under the JParaCrawl license).
- **Quality (paper, their MTEB English benchmark, Avg-s2s / Avg).** Ours-256 63.76 / 51.97; Ours-512 64.06 / 52.35; Model2Vec (potion-base-32M) 62.37 / 51.33; WordLlama 60.40 / 50.23; SimCSE 62.75 / 48.86; MiniLM-L6 65.95 / 56.26. Ablation: word-level PCA 48.1 versus sentence-level PCA 49.9 on MTEB Avg. They do not compare against SIF, uSIF or GloVe.
- **Fit.** The best fit of all modern methods: **word-level vectors that `fse_words@1` can consume** (lowercase differences aside; their vocabulary is case-sensitive with a lowercase fallback, so `fse_words@1` would approximate, not reproduce, their tokenization). Their unsupervised core step, PCA on averaged sentence rows with the top components dropped, is what fse's `fit` already computes: `remove_components@1` from the Gram matrix of sentence rows and `project@1` from the rows after removal. Hub upload: small effort. Reproducing their distillation stage needs a teacher and GPU and is out of scope.

### 2.6 New in 2026

- **SSE, Stable Static Embedding (RikkaBotan, 2026-02-08).** `EmbeddingBag(30522, 512, mode='mean')` followed by a per-dimension learned `c_k * tanh(a_k x_k + b_k)` ("Separable Dynamic Tanh"). NanoBEIR NDCG@10 0.5124 versus 0.5032 for static-retrieval-mrl-en-v1 (model card). Apache-2.0. Source: [model card](https://huggingface.co/RikkaBotan/stable-static-embedding-fast-retrieval-mrl-en). Technical report is a community blog, not peer reviewed. Fit: needs a new non-linear step; `tanh` is not correctly rounded in common libm implementations, so bit-identity across platforms would need fse's own specified tanh. Not worth it for a 0.01 NDCG gain.
- **bekko-static-embedding-v1 (Yuichi Tateno, model 2026-09-14, blog 2026-10-21).** Multilingual mean-pooled static model, custom uncased WordPiece with 200k tokens over 40 languages, 1024 dims with Matryoshka, trained from random initialization with MNRL on about 814M pairs, no teacher. Blog numbers (nDCG@10 x 100): NanoBEIR-en 53.71 (static-similarity-mrl-multilingual-v1 43.51, potion-multilingual-128M 37.07, multilingual-e5-small 58.37); MMTEB Retrieval 49.96 (41.21, 37.86, 50.9); MMTEB Mean 50.18 (47.24, 47.23, 56.4). License on the Hub: MIT (verified via the Hub API; the blog does not state it). Sources: [blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/hotchpotch/bekko-static-embedding), `hotchpotch/bekko-static-embedding-v1`. Fit: like 2.3 (WordPiece, mean, normalize); it ships a custom backend file I did not inspect. These numbers are from the author only and very recent **(not independently reproduced)**.
- **Luxical (DatologyAI, public release v1.1.0 on 2025-11-24).** Not a token table: TF-IDF bag of words (with n-grams), L2-normalized sparse vector, then a small ReLU MLP to a dense normalized vector, distilled from a teacher's similarity matrices. Apache-2.0. No quality numbers in the README. Source: [datologyai/luxical](https://github.com/datologyai/luxical). Fit: needs a non-linear MLP after aggregation; outside the current step list. Not recommended.
- **static-embedding-japanese (hotchpotch, 2025-01-20).** StaticEmbedding (MNRL + Matryoshka), MIT. Japanese tokenizer; relevant only if fse wants Japanese.

The pattern across 2018 to 2020: gains over a strong SIF/uSIF baseline on unsupervised STS are small or absent when good vectors (ParaNMT, PSL) are used (DynaMax Tables 1 and 2; GEM Table 1 shows uSIF above GEM). Gains are larger on supervised classification, where higher dimension (p-means, BOREP) helps.

## 4. Word-vector training architectures after 2017

Evidence, not opinion:

- **New architectures existed but did not become standards.** Sent2Vec (Pagliardini, Gupta, Jaggi, [NAACL 2018](https://aclanthology.org/N18-1049/)) extends CBOW to sentences with n-grams; Spherical Text Embedding/JoSE (Meng et al., [NeurIPS 2019](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/9031-spherical-text-embedding)); Poincaré GloVe ([alex-tifrea/poincare_glove](https://github.com/alex-tifrea/poincare_glove), venue ICLR 2019 **unverified**); probabilistic multi-sense FastText ([benathi/multisense-prob-fasttext](https://github.com/benathi/multisense-prob-fasttext), ACL 2018 **unverified**). Their repositories were last pushed between 2018 and 2023 (GitHub API).
- **The classic tools went into maintenance.** `facebookresearch/fastText` is archived on GitHub (last push 2024-03-22, verified). `tmikolov/word2vec` last push 2023. GloVe was refreshed with the same algorithm: "A New Pair of GloVes" (Carlson, Bauer, Manning, [arXiv 2507.18103](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.18103), July 2025) trains 50 to 300-d vectors on Wikipedia + Gigaword 2024 (vocabulary 1,291,146) and a 300-d model on a Dolma subset (1.2M vocabulary); evaluated on word similarity, analogy and NER, not on sentence tasks. Vectors under the Public Domain Dedication and License (per the GloVe README, as quoted by a search result) **(license statement not read directly; the Stanford site refused connections)**.
- **Static vectors became students of transformers.** Bommasani, Davis, Cardie ([ACL 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.431)) reduce contextual models to static tables; Gupta and Jaggi ([ACL 2021](https://aclanthology.org/2021.acl-long.408)) distill contextual embeddings into static ones with a CBOW extension and report that they beat static embeddings trained from scratch; Model2Vec (2024), Tokenlearn (2024 to 2025), WordLlama (2024) and Wada et al. (EMNLP 2025) all derive their tables from transformers. The 2025 to 2026 Sentence Transformers static models, SSE and bekko are trained from random initialization, but with the contrastive data and losses developed for transformer sentence encoders.
- **The quality frontier is transformer-based.** In every table above a small transformer (all-MiniLM-L6-v2, multilingual-e5-small) beats the best static model: Wada et al. MTEB Avg 52.35 vs MiniLM-L6 56.26; model2vec table 52.13 vs 55.93; NanoBEIR 0.5032 vs 0.5623; bekko MMTEB Mean 50.18 vs 56.4.

**Statement on the claim "after word2vec-style architectures, transformers dominated".** It is accurate for quality: since 2018 no word-vector training architecture displaced word2vec, GloVe or fastText, and the best sentence embeddings are transformers. It is inaccurate as a statement that static embeddings stopped mattering: from 2024 a distinct line of static models appeared that are 100x to 500x faster on CPU (self-reported by the authors) and reach roughly 90 % of a small transformer on MTEB-style averages. The training *method* for static tables changed (distillation and contrastive learning instead of co-occurrence objectives); the inference model stayed the 2015 bag of embeddings with mean pooling.

## 5. Can model2vec vectors be loaded as fse word vectors?

Yes, with the following verified facts and caveats:

1. **Tensors.** potion models store a single F32 `embeddings` tensor (rows = tokenizer vocabulary order), no weights; Sentence Transformers static models store `embedding.weight` in `0_StaticEmbedding/model.safetensors`; WordLlama stores F16 `embedding.weight`. All map to fse `vectors` with the token strings from `tokenizer.json` as `vocab.bytes`. Token strings in WordPiece vocabularies are unique (they are dict keys), satisfying fse's uniqueness rule.
2. **Pooling.** All compute an unweighted mean (model2vec multiplies by `weights` first if present). fse: `weighted_mean@1` with factor 1 (or the stored weights), then `normalize@1` (potion configs set `normalize: true`; for the others normalization does not change cosine rankings).
3. **Tokenizer.** Requires the teacher's Hugging Face tokenizer. With fse's `external` tokenizer this works today, caller-owned. A native `fse` WordPiece identifier would make potion-base-*, potion-retrieval-32M, static-retrieval-mrl-en-v1 and bekko encoders self-contained; Unigram (potion-multilingual-128M) and Llama BPE (WordLlama) would need further identifiers.
4. **Differences that remain by design.** model2vec truncates at 512 tokens by default, fse does not; `[UNK]` handling differs between model2vec (dropped) and Sentence Transformers (kept); summation order differs, so rows agree within float32 rounding.
5. **Do they beat SIF/uSIF on MTEB?** In fse's own baseline (35 tasks, single run), potion-base-8M is ahead of uSIF paranmt-300 in five of seven task types by 3.3 to 4.6 points and ties on STS and Summarization. In model2vec's own table, potion-base-8M (51.08) and potion-base-32M (52.13) are 5 to 6 points above GloVe_300d (45.82); fse's uSIF glove-wiki-gigaword-300 is below uSIF paranmt in fse's baseline on STS (65.35 vs 72.12). Retrieval is the largest gain for the retrieval-trained models (potion-retrieval-32M, static-retrieval-mrl-en-v1); I found no table that puts those two next to uSIF on the same tasks.

## 6. Ranked shortlist for fse 2.x

1. **Import of distilled/trained static tables (model2vec/POTION, Sentence Transformers static, WordLlama) as fse word vectors, encoder `weighted_mean@1` + `normalize@1`.** Largest measured quality gain (fse baseline, section 1), no new aggregation or step, MIT/Apache-2.0 sources compatible with redistribution on the hub, keeps the frozen, bit-reproducible, CPU path. Start with `external` tokens; effort small.
2. **Native WordPiece tokenizer identifier** (BERT uncased normalizer + pre-tokenizer + greedy WordPiece). Turns item 1 into self-contained encoders that run inside fse's GIL-free text pass, covering potion-base-8M/32M, potion-retrieval-32M, static-retrieval-mrl-en-v1 and bekko. Effort medium (Unicode NFD and accent tables, reference in `fse._reference_text`, conformance against Hugging Face `tokenizers`).
3. **Wada et al. (EMNLP 2025) word vectors on the hub.** Word-level, Apache-2.0, beat potion-base-32M on the authors' MTEB benchmark (52.35 vs 51.33), consumable by `fse_words@1` (approximately), and their unsupervised step equals fse's existing `remove_components` + `project` fit. Effort small; requires an fse MTEB run to confirm with `fse_words@1` tokenization.
4. **Zipfian whitening as a `fit` recipe, plus conceptor negation as a weighted `remove_components@1` recipe.** Zipfian whitening shows the largest classic STS-B gain found (GloVe 46.17 to 66.92, above SIF+CCR 58.70); both fold into the word vectors, use the `counts` tensor fse already defines, and add no runtime arithmetic. Effort small. Gains on top of ParaNMT or distilled tables are unmeasured and must be benchmarked.
5. **Matryoshka truncation.** Needed to use the 1024-d MRL models at 256 or 128 dims. Already expressible as `project@1` with unit basis rows + `normalize@1`; a dedicated `truncate@1` step would only save the dot products. Effort small.
6. **`max@1` and a concatenating aggregation (SWEM-concat, p-means with p in {1, ±inf}).** Already planned in docs/architecture/overview.md, "Principle and stages" and fits rule 1 (combine/finish, group mode by max), exact arithmetic. Evidence of benefit is on supervised classification (p-means, SWEM), not on unsupervised STS; DynaMax's larger STS gains need pairwise similarity, which is out of scope. Effort medium.

Not recommended: GEM (per-sentence factorizations, uSIF scores higher in GEM's own table), DynaMax (pairwise), DCT (positional aggregation plus cos reproducibility), P-SIF (document-oriented, K-fold rows), random encoders (no STS gain), SSE's tanh step and Luxical's MLP (non-linear steps with libm reproducibility issues for small gains), and training or distillation inside fse (needs torch, teachers and GPUs; belongs in external tools whose outputs fse loads).
